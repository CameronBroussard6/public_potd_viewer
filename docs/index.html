Record: 191-165, +3.49 units Last Pick: Nishesh Basavareddy ML vs Luka Mikrut (-134, 1 unit) ✅ Tennis | Laver Cup | 3:30 PM EST (esimtated) Today's Pick: Alcaraz/Mensik (Team Europe) vs Fritz/Bublik (Team World) | **Alcaraz/Mensik ML at -132 (FD). 1 unit.** Write-up: Basavareddy ended up winning that match pretty easily and qualifying for the main draw. It’s weird to think that I didn’t post a single pick for the entirety of the US Open main draw, but I only want to post when I see real value, and that can be hard to come by when you’re choosing between the most heavily-scrutinized matches in the sport. I’ve decided to make my return with the Laver Cup starting tomorrow. This event has become a fairly fun yearly tradition - even though Europe generally has a much better lineup on paper, the bulk of the points are available on Day 3, which means that there’s always a chance of World pulling the upset. For this pick, I’m backing Carlos Alcaraz and Jakub Mensik to defeat Taylor Fritz and Alexander Bublik in a doubles match that will cap off the first day of action. Alcaraz may have failed to secure an eighth Grand Slam title and double career Slam at the US Open, but he pretty much quashed any doubts about where his level would be following six months off to rehabilitate his wrist. The loss to Shelton was a bit surprising given the level he had shown in the last couple rounds, but it was exactly the kind of loss you might expect for a player who still has all the skills but is still recovering his match sharpness. Carlos had chances, but Shelton was able to harness the crowd’s energy and deliver a signature performance to take him down in the latest finish in US Open history. Now, he’ll have the chance to reset and kick off the final stretch of the year with a laid-back team event, but I would still expect the effort level to be there to an extent. Last year, Alcaraz went 1-1 in singles and 2-0 in doubles here in London, and he has remarked in a press conference that, “For me especially, I love team events, spending time with them, having fun, as you could see the previous year, how much fun I had on the court and off the court.” The prize money on offer doesn’t hurt either, with $250,000 on offer for each member of the winning team. Of course, you can’t really compare doubles to singles, but we did see Alcaraz and Mensik team up to defeat Fritz and Alex Michelsen in last year’s edition, so they should have some experience in this matchup. Alcaraz might not have a ton of doubles experience, but his skillset is versatile enough that I trust him to deliver a solid performance when motivated. Plus, he won’t have to carry here by any means, because Mensik has a booming serve and some of the best volleys among the younger generation. We saw those skills on full display when he won the 2026 US Open Mixed Doubles title with Karolina Muchova, and he also partnered up with Jiri Lehecka to reach the Brisbane final in 2025. Mensik also played in Czechia’s Davis Cup tie on indoor courts last week (though he only played singles), so he should be prepared for the conditions here. Fritz is actually the most accomplished doubles player on the court with an Olympic bronze medal on his resume, but I’m not sure about the pairing with Bublik. In theory, you would expect two of the best servers on tour to dominate their service games, but the courts here at the Laver Cup tend to be quite slow, and that can lead to some more extended exchanges. Neither of them have top-touch touch or volleys, and there’s also the more intangible factor of team chemistry. Fritz tends to be all business, whereas Bublik likes to mess around a lot more, particularly in lower-stakes matches. One final factor I would like to mention is scheduling. Mensik and Bublik are both serving double duties on Friday, but the difference is that Mensik will play in the day session, while Bublik will play the night session singles match against Rafael Jodar immediately preceding this match. These matches are played with a 10-point tiebreaker in lieu of a third set, so it’s unlikely he comes into this with nothing left in the tank, but I just thought it was worth mentioning.
